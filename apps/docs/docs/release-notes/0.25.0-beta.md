# v0.25.0-beta

## Summary

## Features

### One catalogue of ingredients

Norish now knows that “onion”, “onions”, “onion, diced”, “ui” and “oignon” are one
food. Recipes and grocery lines still read exactly as written, but an aisle, a
product link, a store preference or a pantry item is now about the ingredient, so
you teach Norish each food once instead of once per translation.

- The catalogue starts from the Open Food Facts ingredients taxonomy, fetched
  before a new server first starts serving and refreshed nightly, so it already
  recognises thousands of foods in many languages.
- A new **Ingredients** tab in Settings shows the catalogue in your language. Each
  ingredient opens in a panel with its name, parent and translations. Norish flags the
  ingredients it was not sure about and says why; merge them into the food they are,
  mark them distinct, ask AI to sort them out, move a translation back out, delete
  one nothing uses, or say one food is a kind of another.
- **Ask AI** looks harder than an import does: a flagged name no known food shares
  a word with (“uien”, a brand name) is first named as the plain food it is, and
  compared with the foods that name finds. Its answer says what it tried.
- **AI suggests, you decide.** Ask AI and Find parent with AI never change an
  ingredient themselves: each answer is a suggestion (merge, parent, or a food
  of its own) you confirm or dismiss, one by one or all at once. A suggested
  parent is filled into the ingredient's panel, ready to save.
- Asking AI about every flagged ingredient on screen runs as one background job
  with a step per ingredient. It carries on after the tab is closed, every open
  Ingredients page shows each flagged row waiting its turn, and it shows in the admin job monitor with
  the models it asked. It asks a few ingredients at once, and when it
  ends the **Suggestions** panel opens with what AI proposes for each, and
  why it had nothing for the rest. An ingredient's name there opens its own
  panel.
- An ingredient's panel saves its name, parent and translations together with
  **Save**. The list is a tree of kinds: a food with kinds folds them out beneath
  it; a search or a filter lists flat. A filter shows the ingredients with
  neither parent nor kinds, and **Find parent with AI**, on one ingredient or
  all of those on screen, asks what food each is a kind of. Every edit on the
  page shows the moment it is made: a save, a merge, a delete, a suggestion
  confirmed or dismissed, or a nutrition correction, with the server's answer
  following behind. A refused edit is put back with a word on why.
- Searching the catalogue finds the text anywhere in a name or translation; a
  filters panel beside the box switches to an exact match, searches the parent
  too, or shows only the flagged. The list loads more as you scroll, and
  flagged ingredients lead it.
- A kind of a food uses its parent's aisle and covers it in the pantry: “red
  onion” at home covers a recipe's “onion”.
- Server admins choose who may change an ingredient someone else added, under
  **Settings → Admin → Permissions**.

See [Ingredients](../groceries/ingredients.md).

### Nutrition worked out from the ingredients

A recipe that doesn't state its nutrition now shows calories, fat, carbohydrates
and protein per serving worked out from its own lines, on every server, with or
without AI.

- Each line is counted by its weight, by what one piece of the food weighs
  (“2 onions”) or by what a spoon or cup of it weighs (“1 tsp cumin”, “1 cup
  milk”).
- Spoon measures count without AI. A food whose numbers come from CIQUAL takes
  the spoon weight USDA measured for it, so cumin, butter, breadcrumbs, soy
  sauce and vinegar by the spoon count; spices, sauces, syrups, vinegars and
  creams share one with their kinds, so garam masala and kookroom count too.
- An asterisk on the calories says lines were left out, and **N lines not
  counted** beneath opens every one of them with why: seasoning (a pinch, “to
  taste”), no amount, a measure with no set weight, or a food with no numbers,
  spoon weight or piece weight yet. A line whose fix is in its ingredient's
  panel opens that panel.
- The total says it is estimated when the lines that borrowed from a more
  general food bring a tenth of its calories, and credits the datasets it used.
- The numbers come from CIQUAL 2025 and CALNUT, USDA FoodData Central and CoFID,
  matched to each ingredient through Open Food Facts. An ingredient's panel
  shows its numbers and where they came from, and what a spoon of it weighs in
  the measure your recipes use (“a teaspoon of cumin weighs 2 g”), asking for
  it where Norish doesn't know.
- Your household can **correct** any ingredient's numbers and weights, by
  picking another dataset food or typing them from a label. A correction counts
  for your household only.
- With AI, nutrition estimation now fills only the lines that couldn't be
  counted, seasoning excepted, instead of guessing the whole recipe.
- A line nothing else weighs counts by the weight it states in brackets (“1
  (15 ounce) can coconut milk”, “1 tsp (3 g) paprika”), “2 large eggs” counts
  two eggs, a unit an older recipe left in a line's text (“150 GR
  cherrytomaten”) counts as that unit, and a kind weighs its own size: an egg
  yolk is no whole egg, and a cherry tomato no tomato.

See [Nutrition](../recipes/nutrition.md).

## Fixes and Improvements

- Ingredient lines such as “salt to taste”, “a pinch of nutmeg” and “parsley for
  garnish” are now recognised as salt, nutmeg and parsley. Old recipes with such
  lines are tidied up when the server starts: the ingredients Norish added for
  them are merged into the food they name, or filed under it.
- Preparation written without a comma, as many recipe sites publish it (“2 garlic
  cloves crushed”, “onion finely chopped”, “400g can chickpeas drained and
  rinsed”), is now read past: the line is the food it names, and a new ingredient
  is named without the preparation. A server without AI no longer collects a
  flagged ingredient per such line, and the first start after upgrading renames
  the ones it already has and folds the duplicates together.
- A new ingredient whose name contains a known food is filed under that food
  while it waits for review. “Ground cumin” goes under cumin; “garlic cloves”
  goes under garlic with a suggestion to confirm or dismiss, since the food was
  not the last word. Dismissing takes the parent off again. With AI, the two
  readings are weighed together: agreement files the ingredient without a flag,
  and a Decision Model settles a disagreement.
- A section heading in an ingredient list (“# For the sauce”) is no longer
  added to the catalogue as an ingredient.
- A new ingredient is no longer filed under a food some other language spells
  with an everyday word (Danish's duck is “and”), or under what a food comes
  in: “tuna in olive oil” is tuna, never olive oil.
- A plural or a diminutive of a name Norish knows is that food, in every
  language it speaks: “carrots” is carrot, and “bosuien” and “bosuitjes” are
  “bosui”. A size (“grote ui”) and a quantity an import left at the start of a
  name (“ongeveer 4 el bosui”) are read past too. The words behind this are a
  new setting, **Ingredient words**, under Settings → Admin → Content Detection,
  shipped for every language Norish speaks. See
  [Admin settings](../configuration/admin-settings.md#ingredient-words).
- The Ingredients page shows its AI buttons only where AI is set up, like the
  rest of the app.
- **Mark distinct** is now remembered, so Norish never merges that ingredient
  later.
- Every word in the default units list now names one unit. “1 T olive oil” is a
  tablespoon and “1 t salt” a teaspoon, “stuk” and “Stück” are pieces
  rather than chunks nothing counted, “scheutje” is a splash rather than a dash
  that counted as nothing, “dozen” is a dozen rather than boxes, British
  “heaped” spoons are read, and so are the Dutch “eetl” and “theel”. Lines already saved keep their unit, but an old
  “stuk” saved as a chunk now counts as pieces.
- A cup of whipping cream is weighed as poured, not as whipped cream at half
  the weight, and cream of every kind and ricotta now count by the spoon or
  cup.

## Upgrade notes

- **Ingredients are sorted into the catalogue on first start.** The server first fetches the catalogue, then reads every existing recipe line, grocery, Pantry item, Product Link, Aisle Link and store preference the way it reads a new import, without AI: a line the catalogue knows lands on that food (“knoflook (fijngehakt)” is garlic), and one it doesn't becomes a new ingredient, named without its preparation, flagged for review and filed under the food its words name. Section headings (“# For the sauce”) become no ingredient at all. A server with a long history starts with a long list of flagged ingredients; **Ask AI** works through them. Nothing a household set up is lost.
- **New: `INGREDIENT_CATALOGUE_URL`.** Norish now seeds its ingredient catalogue from the Open Food Facts ingredients taxonomy, fetched at startup and nightly from GitHub. Nothing needs setting; on a server without internet, point it at a local mirror of the file or set it empty to turn the fetch off. See [Server & runtime](../configuration/server-runtime.md#ingredient-catalogue).
- **New: `INGREDIENT_REVIEW_CONCURRENCY`.** How many ingredients a round of Ask AI asks about at once, `10` by default. Lower it if a self-hosted model struggles with parallel requests. See [Server & runtime](../configuration/server-runtime.md#ingredient-catalogue).
- **Nutrition data ships with the release.** The datasets behind ingredient nutrition are part of Norish and are applied when the server starts; nothing is fetched and nothing needs setting. The first start after upgrading also looks at old flagged ingredients once (see Fixes and Improvements).
- **Units list.** A server whose admin never edited the units list takes the new default on its first start. One whose admin edited it keeps its own list, so a word two of its units share still reads by the order the server stores the list in; compare it with the new default to pick up the fixes.
