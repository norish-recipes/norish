# v0.22.0-beta

## Summary

Recipes can now be grouped into **Cookbooks**, and the dashboard becomes your Library: recipes and cookbooks together, under three chips. Sessions are also checked properly now, so an account that is gone can no longer be signed in with.

## Features

### Cookbooks

Recipes can now be grouped into titled sets, and the dashboard becomes your
**Library**: recipes and cookbooks together in one list.

- A **Cookbook** is a title and a set of recipes, and nothing else to
  maintain. Everything its card says is read off the members themselves — the
  cover, a description naming what is inside, their cooking times added up,
  the smallest number of people any of them serves, and any of your allergens
  waiting in there — so there is nothing to upload, nothing to write and
  nothing that goes stale.
- Three chips under the search bar choose what is on screen — All, Recipes,
  Cookbooks — and the heading names the choice. Your chip is remembered on that
  device, and Clear filters leaves it alone.
- Under All, both kinds share one list ordered by the sort you already chose,
  so "Newest first" means newest first for everything. Searching finds a
  cookbook by its title and shows it ahead of the matching recipes.
- File a recipe from **Cookbooks** in its actions menu: one panel lists every
  cookbook you may edit with membership as a toggle, so the same place both
  files and unfiles, and the top row asks for a new cookbook already holding
  the recipe. Nothing is written until you press Save, and a cookbook you ask
  for shows up in the list straight away. Every recipe page ends with a card
  naming the cookbooks it is in — a Hidden Item, so you can turn it off per
  device if you do not use them.
- **Edit cookbook** opens its name and what is in it together, from its card
  in the Library or from the menu on its own page: rename it, mark recipes to
  take out, and Save applies both.
- A cookbook has its own page and address, browsable with your stored grid or
  list layout, your sort, your search and the Filters panel. Making one leaves
  you on the Library rather than opening it, so a cookbook set up for later
  does not interrupt what you were reading.
- Cookbooks work Offline: every one you can see, and what is in it, is part of
  the guaranteed offline set, and making a cookbook or filing into it while
  Offline is queued and arrives intact.
- Nothing here is destructive. Deleting a cookbook leaves its recipes alone,
  deleting a recipe leaves its cookbooks standing, and a cookbook whose owner
  deletes their account stays for the household to maintain.
- **No new setting.** Who may see, rename and delete a cookbook follows the
  recipe permission policy you already configured.
- The mobile app is unchanged this release; cookbooks are web-only for now.

## Fixes and Improvements

- **The way back names where you came from** Every recipe page said "Back to recipes" and went to the dashboard, whoever opened it — so a recipe opened from inside a cookbook offered to take you somewhere you had never been. The link now names the actual origin: the cookbook you came from, or the Library under the lens you left lit. Cookbook pages have the same link, which they did not before.

- **Deleted accounts keep no way in** Sessions are held in Redis, and each one carried a copy of the account as it looked at sign-in. Nothing checked that copy again, so a session stayed usable after the account behind it was gone, and clearing the database left every open session working. Every entry point now resolves the account for real on each request and turns away a session whose account no longer exists, discarding the stale session as it goes.
- **Server admin rights take effect at once** Whether you are a server admin was read from that same sign-in copy, so granting or withdrawing it did nothing until the session expired, up to a week later. It is now read from the account itself, on every request.
- **OIDC no longer demotes the server owner** On a server where OIDC claim mapping is on, the first account was created as owner and admin and then had admin taken straight back off by the same sign-in, unless the token happened to carry the admin group. The owner now keeps admin regardless of the claim.
