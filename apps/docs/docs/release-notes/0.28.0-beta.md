# v0.28.0-beta

## Summary

## Features

### Households and content

Every account belongs to one household, starting with a household of one at sign-up. One Permission Policy controls whether Everyone or only the Household can view, edit and delete recipes and cookbooks. Server admins follow the same policy.

Tags, Cuisines, Categories and the ingredient catalogue belong to your household. Your lists also include the server's Cuisines, built-in Categories and the shared ingredient seed. Tags, Cuisines and Categories stay until deleted or merged. The recipe editor offers **Create** for new entries. Imports can add Tags and Categories. AI can add Tags and Cuisines when configured, and ingredient text can add foods.

Open **Content** in Settings on web or mobile to add, rename, merge and delete entries and see **Used by**. On mobile, long-press an editable list entry for its menu.

When you leave a household, your recipes and cookbooks go with you, along with copies of the Tags, Cuisines, Categories and foods your recipes use. Groceries, pantry, stores and the meal plan stay behind. A household admin hands over the role before leaving. Joining brings your groceries, pantry, stores, meal plan, lists and foods into the household you join. Deleting your account erases the recipes and cookbooks you own. A planned meal whose recipe is erased becomes a note. A household of one is deleted with its account. See [Households](/households).

### Settings in the iPhone app

Open **All settings** from your avatar to manage your profile, preferences,
allergies, household and connections. You can also change server, open support,
see version information and sign out.

## Fixes and Improvements

- A share link stops working when its maker can no longer view the recipe.
- CalDAV removes the meal plan entries left behind from a departing member's calendar.
- An import skips Cuisines that neither the server nor the household has.
- On iPhone, you can copy an active recipe share link made in 0.28 or later from Settings.
- On the web, Norish tells you when a profile save is refused because the profile changed elsewhere.
- On the web, failed household and recipe share-link actions show a message.
- On the web, the API key name field stops at 32 characters and the household name field at 100.
- The web app reconnects faster after the browser comes back online.
- New API keys and recipe share links are no longer stored for replay after creation.

## Upgrade notes

- Back up the database before upgrading. Installing the old version cannot undo the migration.
- The upgrade changes `owner` to Household, fills missing permission levels, limits edit and delete to no wider than view, and removes the ingredient permission setting automatically.
- Ownerless recipes and cookbooks go to the server owner, or the oldest admin, or the oldest account, and join that account's household. If there are no accounts, they are deleted. Conflicting recipe URLs are cleared.
- Each Tag and custom Category stays with the household of its oldest recipe and is copied to other households that use it. Unused Tags and custom Categories are removed.
- Each non-seed food stays with the household of the account that added it, or the fallback account above, and is copied to other households that use it.
- Existing Cuisines, built-in Categories and seeded foods stay shared. Names that fold alike are merged at the first start.
- Server admins no longer bypass the Permission Policy to see other households' recipes. Share-link moderation, the job monitor and admin-started bulk AI enrichment still reach across households.
- With OIDC claim mapping enabled, a household claim merges a household of one into the household with that name, or renames it if none matches. An account in a shared household stays there. The name `My household` is ignored.
- The admin `?scope=instance` recipe export has been removed. Recipe archives contain recipes the account can view.
- The ingredient catalogue export contains the seed and your household's foods.
