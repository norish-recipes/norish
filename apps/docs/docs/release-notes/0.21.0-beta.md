# v0.21.0-beta

## Summary

This release gives the whole product one warm look, from the sign-in page to
the app itself, introduces Hidden Items for slimming your view of a recipe,
and replaces the browser behind recipe URL imports.

## Features

### One warm ground for the whole product

The app now stands on the same warm ground as norish.dev: a soft beige page
behind white cards in light, a warm brown in dark, with borders, separators
and muted text warmed to match. Body text and headings stay neutral and
high-contrast, so the warmth never costs legibility.

Along with the ground, the details were brought in line:

- **Chips follow one rule.** A chip that names a thing, a tag, a category, a
  cuisine, is filled in the accent color when active and a soft warm fill when
  not. A chip that reports a condition, such as a failed import, keeps its
  semantic color, because there the color is the information.
- **Fake glass is gone.** Blurred, see-through surfaces imitating glass have
  left the app entirely. Floating chrome, overlays on photos and everything
  in between is now drawn as a real, opaque surface: easier to read over any
  background and cheaper to scroll.
- **The bottom bar is one solid object.** On mobile the bar is a single
  surface with your account avatar as a fourth item and a filled pill marking
  where you are.
- **Refreshed controls.** The component library moved to HeroUI 3.2.4, and
  segmented controls such as the library's grid and list switch now sit on its
  built-in tabs, with the same look everywhere.

### A sign-in page that belongs to the product

Signing in now looks like arriving at Norish rather than at a bare form: the
warm ground, the wordmark standing on its own, a serif heading and the small
ingredient drawings from norish.dev in the margins. Sign-in failures are now
proper alerts with an icon and a title instead of a red sentence under the
form.

### Hidden Items

One control in Settings → User holds everything you would rather not be
shown: Recipe Provenance, Nutrition Information, notes, ratings, favorites,
the measurement conversion control and recipe timers. Everything is shown by
default; hiding an item suppresses it everywhere it would appear for you, and
for you alone. What is stored on the recipe, and what other household members
see, does not change. The origin flag beside a recipe's title stays even when
Recipe Provenance is hidden. See [Hidden Items](../recipes/hidden-items.md).

Your choices follow you between devices, and the app remembers them from the
very first frame: a hidden section never flashes into view on load. The same
holds for your other view preferences, such as the groceries view, the
Today section's visibility and fraction or decimal amounts.

## Fixes and Improvements

- **URL imports render in Obscura instead of a third-party Chromium container.**
  The `chrome-headless` service is replaced by `obscura`, a browser image the
  Norish project publishes itself, pinned to one exact upstream release, for
  both x86-64 and ARM. It starts in stealth mode every time, so an import gets
  the same treatment on every instance, and it still refuses to fetch addresses
  inside your own network. Pages that used to need a lucky retry to get past a
  site's bot screening should behave better; pages that never worked may still
  not. See [Recipe parser](../configuration/parser.md) — and the Upgrade notes
  below, because the setting was renamed.

## Upgrade notes

- **The individual display switches were replaced by Hidden Items.** The
  separate settings switches for ratings, favorites, ingredient conversion
  and recipe timers are gone; the new Hidden Items control covers all of them
  and more. There is deliberately no migration: everything starts visible, so
  if you had ratings, favorites or timers hidden you will need to hide them
  once more in the new control.

- **Breaking: `CHROME_WS_ENDPOINT` is replaced by `OBSCURA_ENDPOINT`, and the
  `chrome-headless` service by `obscura`.** There is no alias and no deprecation
  period — an unchanged compose file leaves URL imports pointing at a browser
  that is no longer there. In your compose file, replace the `chrome-headless`
  service with:

  ```yaml
  obscura:
    image: norishapp/obscura:0.2.0-norish.1
    restart: unless-stopped
  ```

  then swap `CHROME_WS_ENDPOINT: ws://chrome-headless:3000` for
  `OBSCURA_ENDPOINT: ws://obscura:9222` in the `norish` service and update any
  `depends_on` entry. The image needs no command, no flags, and no shared-memory
  tuning; it does not publish a port, and it should not — anything that can
  reach it can drive the browser. If you run the browser on another host, point
  `OBSCURA_ENDPOINT` at that Obscura CDP server instead.
