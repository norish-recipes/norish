# v0.20.0-beta

## Summary

This release makes the Norish web app work offline, and gives administrators
one clear set of controls over the AI work Norish does on your recipes. Pages you rely on keep
working when your server is unreachable — on flaky Wi-Fi, on the move, or
while the backend restarts — and changes you make while offline are saved on
your device and sent automatically once the server is back. The release also
hardens account switching on shared browsers.

## Features

### One predictable flow for AI recipe enrichment

- Every recipe now enters the same enrichment flow after it is saved, whether
  you typed it in yourself or imported it from a link, a photo, or pasted text.
  Previously what you got depended on how the recipe entered Norish: a link
  import that AI had to read skipped the background work entirely, pasted
  recipes got two of the four, image imports got none, and a recipe you typed
  in got nothing at all.
- Saving comes first and is final. Enrichment is enrolled separately and cannot
  delay a save, undo one, or turn a successful import into a failure.
- **Four independent switches** under **Settings → Admin → AI**: auto-tagging,
  allergy detection, auto-categorization, and nutrition estimation. Each decides
  only whether that kind runs automatically for new recipes.
- Household members can still run any of the four by hand from a recipe's
  actions menu whenever AI is enabled and they can edit the recipe — turning off
  a background switch no longer takes away the tool.
- **Your data wins.** Categories or nutrition you supplied — or that an import
  source stated explicitly — are never replaced by automatic enrichment, even if
  you fill them in while it is running. Tags and allergy indications are only
  ever added, so nothing you organized is removed, and two enrichments finishing
  at the same time can no longer lose each other's findings.
- Each kind reports its own state on the recipe — queued, processing, succeeded,
  or failed — and recovers correctly after a refresh or a dropped connection.
  Successful enrichment simply updates the recipe, with no notification; a run
  you asked for tells you if it fails, while automatic runs stay quiet.
- **Tag strategy** is now separate from whether auto-tagging runs automatically,
  so turning the automation off no longer discards your chosen strategy.

### Recipe provenance: where a recipe comes from

- Norish can now work out **where a recipe comes from** and show it on the
  recipe page — beside the media on a desktop, and right after the **Cook**
  button on a phone: the country as the section's own heading, a finer-grained
  region when the dish warrants one, the cuisines it belongs to, and a short
  written explanation. The country's flag also flies in front of the recipe's
  title and beside every recipe on your dashboard.
- The country's name and the explanation are written in **the language the
  recipe itself is written in** — an Italian recipe gets an Italian note beside
  its Italian steps, and a Dutch recipe about a Turkish dish is titled
  _Turkije_ — because both are recipe content, not interface text, and are
  never translated. Pick a country by hand and the label you picked is stored
  in your own words. Interface chrome — the flag's tooltip, the dashboard, the
  country picker — stays in your language.
- **A contested dish still gets a country.** When several countries claim a
  dish, inference picks the single strongest claim and acknowledges the rivals
  in the note; only a dish that belongs to no national tradition at all keeps
  an empty country.
- **Cuisines are now a curated list your administrator owns**, under
  **Settings → Admin → AI & Processing → Cuisines**: add, rename, or remove
  entries without waiting for a Norish release. Renaming updates every recipe
  using it at once. A new **Cuisine strategy** setting decides whether AI may
  only pick from your list or may also add to it; it defaults to picking only.
- Provenance is a fifth kind of AI enrichment, with its own switch under
  **Settings → Admin → AI**, and its own editable prompt. **It starts switched
  off.** You can ask for a run yourself at any time from a recipe's actions menu,
  whether or not the switch is on.
- **Your own answer wins, as one group.** Fill in any part of provenance — just
  the country, just a cuisine, just the note — and automatic inference skips that
  recipe entirely. A run you ask for replaces the whole group. In the recipe form
  each field empties on its own: pick **No country**, clear the region or note,
  or deselect every cuisine.
- Provenance travels into your offline copy with the recipe, so an offline reader
  sees exactly what a connected one does.

### Step ingredients: each step shows what it uses

- A step can now carry the ingredient lines it uses, shown beneath the step as
  resolved names and amounts — on the recipe page, in cooking mode, and on
  share links. _"Add the spices"_ lists the salt, the pepper, and the paprika;
  _"add half the water"_ reads **25 ml water** when the line is 50 ml, so the
  arithmetic is never yours to do mid-cook.
- Amounts are computed at the moment you read them, from the live ingredient
  line: they follow every edit, the active measurement system, and the
  servings control. A line with no amount shows its name only.
- In the recipe form, every step has a chips row: type `@` to mention an
  ingredient — the plain word lands in your sentence, the link becomes a chip —
  or attach lines from the picker without naming them in the text. Each chip
  can carry how much of its line the step uses — half, a third, a quarter, a
  custom share, or an exact amount when the line has one: a recipe that calls
  for 5 eggs can use **3** in one step and **2** in another, typed as amounts.
  A tap removes a chip, and your step text is stored exactly as you wrote it.
- Attaching a line that has an amount asks for the amount right away — from
  the `@` mention and the picker alike. Answer and you are back in your
  sentence; Escape or clicking on keeps the whole line, so the ask never costs
  a keystroke you didn't want to spend.
- An entered amount is kept as the equivalent share of its line, so it scales
  with the servings control and follows the measurement system like every
  other step ingredient.
- The old render-time `@`-token linking in step text has been retired in its
  favour. Legacy `@` tokens in stored recipes now read as the literal text they
  always were; nothing is migrated or rewritten.
- Step ingredients travel into your offline copy with the recipe, like
  everything else on it.

### Ingredient Linking: AI fills the steps you haven't linked

- **Link Ingredients to Steps** in a recipe's actions menu asks AI to work out,
  per step, which ingredient lines the step uses and how much of each —
  aggregates, fractions, and stated amounts (_"crack 3 of the eggs"_)
  included.
- It is a **gap-filler in every case**: automatic or requested by hand, it only
  ever adds links to steps that have none, so the links you attached yourself
  are never replaced by a rerun. Section headings are never linked, and a step
  that genuinely uses nothing stays bare.
- It is a sixth kind of AI enrichment, with its own switch under
  **Settings → Admin → AI** and its own editable prompt. **It starts switched
  off.** Automatic failures stay quiet; a run you asked for reports its failure
  and can be run again.

### Web app works offline

- The web app now keeps a guaranteed offline set on your device: your 50 most
  recent recipes in full (including each recipe's main photo), all groceries
  and recurring groceries, your stores, and the calendar's initial window. It
  is refreshed automatically in the background whenever the app is connected.
- Changes made while offline — adding or ticking groceries, editing recipes,
  planning meals, importing from a URL, uploads included — are queued safely
  on the device and sent automatically when the server is reachable again.
  The app only reports a change as "Queued" after it is durably stored.
- You can open the app while your server is down — even pages you had not
  visited in that browser yet. The dashboard, warmed recipe details,
  groceries, and calendar boot straight from the offline copy; anything
  outside the offline guarantee shows a clear "offline unavailable" screen
  instead of a broken page.
- A Live/Offline indicator in the user menu opens a status view showing the
  connection state, what is cached, and any queued changes, with retry and
  discard controls. Failed syncs are parked visibly — never silently dropped
  — and conflicting edits follow a first-writer-wins rule you can reapply by
  hand.
- Duplicate-safe syncing: every queued change carries an operation id, so a
  change is applied at most once even if it has to be resent.

### Safer sign-out and account switching

- Signing out with unsynced offline changes now opens a confirmation that
  explains what would be discarded. Cancel keeps everything; signing out
  clears the queued changes and this device's offline copy of your account.
- If the signed-in account changes outside that dialog (for example in
  another tab on a shared browser), the previous account's offline data and
  images are hidden immediately and its queued changes are kept dormant —
  they sync only after that account signs in again, and never as anyone else.

## Fixes and Improvements

### Offline

- Previously cached groceries, calendar plans, recipes, and profile details now
  remain available after a background refresh fails while your server is
  unreachable.
- A grocery you check off while your server is unreachable stays checked. It
  could previously un-check itself when you left the page and came back, because
  a stale saved copy of the server's answer was served in place of the real one.
  That affected everything you could see offline, not just groceries: offline
  pages now show what your device saved, and never an out-of-date reply passed
  off as a fresh one.

### Recipes and imports

- On phones, the provenance section now sits right after the **Cook** button,
  before the ingredients — where it frames the recipe the way it does on
  desktop — and the doubled rule that could appear above it is gone: the page
  now draws exactly one rule between sections.
- Recipes you create while offline become offline-available immediately,
  without waiting for the next background refresh.
- Importing a recipe from a URL while offline now shows the friendly
  "Queued" notice instead of an error, and runs when you are back online.

### Mobile

- Fixed the mobile app build after the Expo SDK update.

## Upgrade notes

- **API change: `POST /recipes/import/url` now returns an object.** It used to
  answer with a bare recipe id, and with `409 Conflict` when you already had the
  recipe. It now returns `{ "recipeId": "…", "status": "queued" | "exists" }` —
  `exists` carrying the id of the recipe you already hold, so a client can open
  it instead of reporting a failure. A `409` now means only that an import of
  that URL is already in flight. Scripts that read the response as a plain
  string need updating; the browser extension is updated to match.
- **AI enrichment settings migrate automatically, without changing what runs
  today.** The old auto-tagging mode becomes an auto-tagging switch plus a tag
  strategy: `disabled` becomes automation off (keeping "Predefined tags only" as
  the strategy for manual runs), and every other mode becomes automation on with
  the same strategy. The old allergy-tagging setting becomes the allergy
  detection switch as-is. The migration runs at startup; no action is needed.
- **Auto-categorization and nutrition estimation are new automatic controls and
  start switched off**, for existing and new installations alike, so upgrading
  cannot silently increase your AI usage or spend. Turn them on under
  **Settings → Admin → AI** when you want them. Both remain available to run by
  hand regardless.
- Recipe imports no longer ask the AI provider to infer tags, allergies,
  categories, or nutrition while reading a source. Facts a source states
  explicitly are still imported; anything beyond that now follows your automatic
  switches. If you relied on link or image imports arriving pre-tagged, enable
  the matching switches.
- **Cuisines have moved out of the predefined tag list.** Ten cuisines were part
  of it — `italian`, `thai`, `mexican`, and so on — and are now entries in the
  cuisine list instead. Upgrading moves them across automatically: a recipe
  tagged `italian` gains the **Italian** cuisine and loses the tag, and what was
  removed is recorded in the database. Only tags matching the shipped cuisine
  list are touched; cuisine-like tags you typed yourself, such as `sicilian` or
  `tex-mex`, stay exactly where they are. Auto-tagging no longer proposes
  cuisines.
- **Recipe Provenance starts switched off**, for existing and new installations
  alike, so upgrading cannot silently increase your AI usage or spend. Turn it on
  under **Settings → Admin → AI** when you want it; it remains available to run
  by hand regardless.
- **Ingredient Linking starts switched off** for the same reason. Turn it on
  under **Settings → Admin → AI** when you want new recipes linked
  automatically; the manual run in the actions menu works regardless.
- **Inline `@`-token linking in step text is retired.** Steps that contain
  literal `@ingredient` tokens render them as plain text from now on; the links
  themselves now live beside the step as step ingredients. Nothing is migrated:
  re-attach with the editor's chips, or run **Link Ingredients to Steps** on
  recipes whose steps have no links yet.
- No new environment variables.
- The `LEGACY_RECIPE_PARSER_ROLLBACK` setting has been removed. The
  structured recipe parser has been the default since its rollout; if you
  still set this variable, delete it from your environment.
- No manual database steps: schema migrations run automatically at startup,
  as usual.
