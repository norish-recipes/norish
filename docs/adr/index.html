Norish Architecture Decisions

Every decision as its ADR now reads, by area. The Markdown files in docs/adr/ are authoritative; this page only summarises them.

AI

  • ADR-0014 — Norish never claims to know which parameters a model accepts

    The unpopulated capability table is deleted; a refused Generation Preference is dropped at runtime. Rejects maintaining a table of model ids.

  • ADR-0015 — Norish issues every model request through one runtime

    The caller-less executor is deleted and rebuilt as the AI Runtime: one seam for structured generation and transcription, typed thrown errors carrying retryability, enrichment features in shared-server, extraction with the parser. Rejects adopting the executor, a dedicated AI package, and a result union.

  • ADR-0016 — Prompts are appended to, never interpolated

    The runtime accepts a prompt name plus appended sections, never a finished string, so a hardcoded prompt is unrepresentable; new input arrives appended so customised prompts survive upgrades. Rejects placeholder templates for new content and image extraction's string surgery.

  • ADR-0017 — The prompts row stores only overrides

    The stored prompts row carries only texts that differ from the shipped defaults; reads merge over the shipped files, saves strip default-equal fields, and boot prunes legacy rows against every default any release shipped. Rejects seeded default copies and the row-level isOverridden freeze.

  • ADR-0024 — Image generation is the runtime's third entry point, on its own provider

    The AI Runtime has generateImage beside structured generation and transcription, on its own provider block (OpenAI, Google, Azure, Ollama, LM Studio or a generic OpenAI-compatible endpoint), because the other providers expose no image model; endpoint and key fall back to the AI config when the provider matches. One feature, two requests: a structured call writes an English visual brief and the image model draws from it.

  • ADR-0035 — Decisions are the runtime's fourth entry point, on their own provider

    decide is the AI Runtime's fourth entry point, with its own Decision Model block, no key fallback and its own switch apart from the global AI one. A Decision has no Prompt: its options come from the domain (the household's Categories, Cuisines and allergens). Thresholds are constants in code, Decision Uses are an admin multi-select with every use on by default, and every kind falls back to its language-model or heuristic path.

Cookbooks

  • ADR-0026 — The library is one interleaved list, not two

    Recipes and cookbooks share one union, one sort and one pagination, rendered from a discriminated card type, so the All / Recipes / Cookbooks chips are a plain filter rather than a layout switch. Search is the exception: cookbooks match on title alone and are pinned above the ts_rank-ordered recipes, because the two scores come from documents of different shape and length. Rejects two banded queries, pinning cookbooks first in the sort, one comparable rank, and matching a cookbook by its members.

  • ADR-0027 — Cookbooks reuse the recipe permission policy

    A Cookbook belongs to its Owner like a recipe and is governed by the one Permission Policy (ADR-0041), with no rule or setting of its own. Filing a recipe needs view on the recipe and edit on the cookbook and never writes the recipe. A member the reader cannot see is hidden and returns when the policy widens; deleting an account erases its cookbooks.

Groceries

  • ADR-0028 — Store pages are read with a structured-data ladder, not a model

    Store pages are read with a fixed structured-data ladder, never a model: ItemList JSON-LD plus a DOM pass for prices on a results page, Product JSON-LD on a product page, a plain fetch first and a Page Renderer when the shop blocks it or nothing is found. A grocery links to a product unasked only on an exact name or a slip of the fingers; otherwise a Decision may pick (ADR-0035) or the shopper chooses. A product's only per-unit price is its Shelf Price.

  • ADR-0029 — Line Cost is pack arithmetic over what the shop presents

    Line Cost is whole packs at the Shelf Price, rounded up: a bare number is packs, or pieces where the shop counts in pieces, and loose goods are priced by weight. A per-unit price that is a product's only price is its Shelf Price (ADR-0028), a chosen Purchase Amount overrides the arithmetic (ADR-0030), a count needs no Pack Size, and a measure against a product without one costs one pack and says so.

  • ADR-0030 — Shoppers override the purchase amount

    A shopper's optional Purchase Amount is stored on the grocery for this trip only and leaves the requirement intact; clearing it restores the calculation, and a create with an override is never merged. The grocery panel sets it and a row shows it times the Shelf Price. Pack Size is the product's fact and converts a requirement into products.

  • ADR-0031 — An Aisle is a fact about an Ingredient at a Store, not about a list line

    An Aisle Link is a Store, an Ingredient and one of the Store's Aisles, keyed by Ingredient like a Product Link; a grocery's aisle is worked out from it and nothing is stored on the row. Filing one spelling files the food at that Store, an unfiled food sits in its nearest parent's Aisle, and Norish never guesses an Aisle from words.

  • ADR-0036 — Nothing about the Pantry is stored on the recipe or the grocery

    A Pantry Ingredient is a household fact, and whether a line is in the pantry is worked out on read by ADR-0037's rule: the same food, or a kind of it. It is checked when a recipe is added to the groceries, where stocked lines are shown apart and left off unless ticked, and by the Library filter, never on the list itself. A kept food is on the list while a Grocery of that same food is still to buy. The Pantry has its own router and subscription and no REST endpoint.

  • ADR-0037 — Ingredient identity is an alias pointing at an Ingredient

    An Ingredient is one food known by many Ingredient Aliases, and everything that names a food points at an alias; a spelling means one food within a household's catalogue (ADR-0042), and links are keyed by food. One resolver mints, by the Ingredient Words alone: exact, then stripped of amounts, sizes and preparation or as a plural, then the words before a note, then a slip of the fingers, and only then a new food. AI reviews a minted food afterwards where an administrator allows it; its answers are applied, each can be undone, and only an unsure one flags the food.

  • ADR-0038 — The ingredient catalogue is seeded from Open Food Facts under the ODbL

    The catalogue is seeded nightly from the Open Food Facts ingredients taxonomy, every entry and language, with each entry's nutrition codes, piece weight and density. The seed is every household's and the administrator's to edit; a refresh never undoes what a household taught or touches a household's own food. The data is under the ODbL, and the export is the seed plus the reader's household's own foods.

  • ADR-0039 — Ingredient Nutrition comes from vendored open datasets, corrected per household

    Nutrition numbers come from CIQUAL, CALNUT and USDA, joined through the Open Food Facts taxonomy's codes plus exact name matches, in a committed table a monthly workflow rebuilds; Norish keeps fixes, density fixes, piece fixes and a list of parents that never lend. A correction belongs to a household, a recipe's worked-out nutrition is computed on read for the reader's household, and AI estimates the lines left out where allowed.

  • ADR-0042 — Each household has its own catalogue on a shared seed

    A household's catalogue is the seed plus its own foods, spellings and junk marks. Resolution, merges and AI never cross households, a household's spelling outranks the seed's for it, and a seed refresh never touches a household's own food.

Households

  • ADR-0041 — The household is the permission boundary

    Every account is in exactly one household, and household-owned rows carry its id. One Permission Policy, Everyone or Household for view, edit and delete, governs who outside a household reaches its recipes and cookbooks; nothing else a household keeps crosses. A server administrator is an ordinary member under it. Recipes and cookbooks leave with their Owner and are erased with their account.

Imports

  • ADR-0019 — Page Renderers are tried in a fixed order, and the solver never gets tokens

    Every caller fetches plainly first, then tries Obscura, then a Chromium browser, then a FlareSolverr-compatible solver, each switched on in admin settings; a renderer fails on empty HTML or a bot check. Site Auth Tokens go to Obscura and the Chromium browser and never to the solver, and an Allow private addresses switch, off by default, guards the plain fetch, the Chromium browser and the solver. Rejects one engine only, racing, per-site routing, cookies through FlareSolverr, Camoufox, and licence-gated browsers.

  • ADR-0022 — Recipe Archives are portability, not backup

    A Recipe Archive moves recipe content between instances and is not a backup: the importer owns what it creates, and no personal data or accounts travel. An export holds what the exporter may view under the Permission Policy. Cuisines attach only where the importing household's list has them, cookbooks travel as titles matched or created among the importer's own, and an archive never overwrites its own recipes by name.

Offline

  • ADR-0001 — Web offline support is a persisted query cache, not a domain database

    Offline support persists the TanStack Query cache and fills the Warm Set with a Cache Warmer, instead of keeping a separate domain database. Web and mobile share one offline engine in @norish/shared-react/offline, each supplying only its storage adapter, and every adapter passes one shared storage contract suite. The cache is stored one entry per read, saved when idle and only for reads that changed, with a 7-day maxAge counted from the newest save.

  • ADR-0002 — Replay idempotency via a generic operation-id middleware

    Use one path-agnostic operation-id middleware and Redis TTL claims to make Replay duplicate-safe.

  • ADR-0003 — Client-minted entity ids on create

    Create procedures accept an optional client-minted UUID and insert with it, so queued create-then-edit chains stay valid: groceries, recurring groceries, stores and their aisles, Pantry Ingredients, calendar items, recipes and cookbooks. A grocery create the server merges into an existing row is outside that: a queued edit of it fails at Replay and is surfaced. Replay never predicts merges or rewrites ids.

  • ADR-0004 — First-writer-wins on Replay conflicts

    Stale writes park visibly as Conflicted; field-wise conflict merging remains deferred.

  • ADR-0005 — Offline security posture: device possession = read access

    Device possession grants read access: the Offline Cache works across cookie expiry until Session Expiry, which clears it and keeps that Account's Outbox, and it is not encrypted at rest. Sign-out ends the session first, then the device forgets the cache; queued work is discarded only after the reader confirms. An account switch purges the outgoing user's reads and images before rendering, their Outbox stays dormant under its owner, and Replay checks the live session owner.

  • ADR-0006 — Serwist owns the service worker

    Serwist owns the service worker, built with @serwist/turbopack as a static route at /serwist/sw.js, and its content-hashed manifest replaces hand-rolled versioning. A navigation with no cached copy falls back to the precached /~offline shell, which boots the Warm Set surface or Offline-unavailable. Images are cache-first in one bounded cache the Cache Warmer also fills, and same-origin /api/ GETs are never cached.

  • ADR-0007 — Offline-forced is a development-only debug affordance, not a user feature

    The debug toggle faithfully blocks transport and does not ship as a production user feature.

  • ADR-0008 — Newly created recipes join the Warm Set on create

    A newly created recipe is durably offline-available without waiting for the next warm pass.

  • ADR-0009 — Offline guarantees are end-to-end

    The small offline promise holds end to end: Queued means durably stored and a failed admission rolls back, FormData uploads round-trip through the Outbox, unseen Warm Set routes boot through the normal providers while others show Offline-unavailable, warmed recipes include their primary image, sign-out with queued work asks first, and account transitions isolate cached data. Groceries guarantee create and check-off, with no client-side merge parity.

  • ADR-0011 — Recovery reconciles after terminal Replay

    Neither realtime events nor the Resume buffer is the consistency guarantee: after Replay reaches a terminal state, Recovery refetches active queries once and tops up the Warm Set. It runs on startup, return from Offline, reconnect, a Live verdict with pending work, and manual sync; startup and pending-work runs refetch only if Replay sent something. The connectivity loop's cadence is the only retry timer.

  • ADR-0013 — Document navigations observe the Reachability Deadline

    Document navigations observe the same five-second Reachability Deadline as the health probe. A custom service-worker handler races the navigation against it; past the deadline a visited route serves its cached copy and an uncached one falls back to the Warm Set surface or the Offline-unavailable state. Boundedness over freshness: the surfaces refetch once the probe succeeds.

Realtime

  • ADR-0032 — Realtime is one hub, one catalogue, one idiom

    The old realtime layer is deleted, not adapted. Every Realtime Event is declared once, client-safely, with its Scope and zod schema; published through one validated domain object that never throws to its caller; fanned out by one per-process Realtime Hub with a single Redis subscriber and bounded queues; consumed through one subscription factory that yields Envelopes. A subscriber that falls behind is Lagged and refetches. Rejects migration behind shims, per-connection subscribers, pattern subscriptions, publish-time booleans and raw publishing.

  • ADR-0033 — A scope change restarts the socket

    A subscription's identity is fixed at start. Household and account changes publish their announcing event, awaited, then an internal invalidation that closes the user's sockets with 4000; everything restarts against the current identity and a Cursor minted under the old one is refused. Rejects re-keying live subscriptions.

  • ADR-0034 — Resume is a bounded buffer, not a guarantee

    Every non-internal publish is also appended, in one atomic script, to a per-channel Redis Stream of about a thousand entries kept for a day. Subscriptions carry an opaque, identity-bound Cursor, and a reconnect replays what was missed before going live; a bad or expired cursor ends the subscription Lagged and the client refetches. Recovery still runs on every reconnect (ADR-0011).

Recipes

  • ADR-0012 — Cuisines and Tags share a shape and differ in governance

    Cuisines and Tags each have a table and a join and differ in their write rules. Tags are an open folksonomy; Cuisines are a curated list, the administrator's that every household is offered plus the household's own (ADR-0043). AI extends Cuisines only under the permissive strategy and only in the recipe's household, and proposed names are always matched against the list. Names show verbatim, never translated, and Other is not a Cuisine.

  • ADR-0018 — Automatic provenance fills the group's gaps

    Automatic Recipe Provenance keeps every supplied slot and fills only the absent ones; inference receives the supplied slots as settled facts, and only a complete group suppresses the run. Rejects whole-group suppression by any single value, merging blind to the model, and append-merging supplied Cuisines.

  • ADR-0025 — A Generated Image takes the recipe's primary slot

    A Generated Image is always order 0, and writing one deletes the row and file that held that slot; a recipe holds at most one. Eligibility is the counterweight — automatic runs and default sweeps skip any recipe that already has an image, so only a manual action or an explicit overwrite sweep can reach stored media. Rejects demoting instead of deleting, sparing supplied photographs, and a confirmation on the manual action.

  • ADR-0040 — Categories are household-scoped, not instance-wide

    Categories are rows with a join, not an enum. The four built-ins are fixed for everyone, and a household adds its own, kept until a member deletes one (ADR-0043). A reader is offered the built-ins plus their household's Categories whatever the policy; AI never creates one, other apps' imports keep unmatched names as Tags, a Norish Recipe Archive carries its Categories over, and meal plan slots keep their own enum.

  • ADR-0043 — Tags, Cuisines and Categories are household lists

    Tags, Cuisines and Categories are a household's rows, unique on the folded name, beside instance entries (the built-in Categories and the administrator's Cuisines); a recipe carries only its household's. Entries are added explicitly and kept until deleted, and households may add Cuisines.

Releases

  • ADR-0010 — Release notes use a release checkpoint

    A thin docs-version command, practical release checkpoint, usual-style notes, and production docs validation.

UI

  • ADR-0020 — Norish does not fake glass

    Every backdrop-blur and see-through fill is removed from the web app; anything that floats is an opaque object with a surface fill, border and shadow, and the four cssGlassBackdrop* tokens are deleted so there is nothing left to reach for. Rejects the narrower rule of keeping blur over photos and video, which is the concession that seeded the pattern everywhere else. Scoped to the web: native platform blur is a real compositor effect and is untouched.

  • ADR-0021 — Avatar URLs are versioned and immutable

    An avatar upload mints a new filename and the route serves it immutable, so a changed picture is a changed URL and every cache is correct by construction; the predecessor file survives until the next upload's cleanup and a memberProfileUpdated household event nudges live clients to converge. Rejects per-call-site ?v= cache-busting and a never-cache route, which traded staleness for a guaranteed loading flash and nothing offline.

  • ADR-0023 — Recipe pages take their hue from the dish

    One Dish Colour is extracted server-side from a recipe's primary image and kept on the recipe; the page container scopes it as hue and clamped saturation only, so lightness stays with the reader's theme and contrast is unchanged by construction. Rejects browser-side extraction on render, using the colour directly with luminance-chosen text, and tinting the page while leaving cards neutral. Not a reversal of ADR-0020: a tinted surface is still an opaque object.
