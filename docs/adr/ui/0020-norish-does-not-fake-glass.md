# Norish does not fake glass

Norish's web chrome had drifted into imitating a translucent material: `backdrop-blur` behind a half-opaque fill in roughly twenty-five places — the bottom nav, the timer dock, panels, grocery list headers, empty states, and the chips floating on recipe photos, with four shared `cssGlassBackdrop*` tokens encouraging more of it. The imitation never lands on the web. There is no depth, no refraction and no lighting model to sell it; a blur pass over a flat page background yields a muddy wash rather than a material, it is expensive to composite on a scrolling phone, and it leaves text contrast at the mercy of whatever happens to pass underneath. **Every `backdrop-blur` and every see-through fill is removed from `apps/web`.** Anything that floats is a real object drawn from the token set — a surface fill, a border, a shadow. We rejected the narrower rule of keeping blur only where there is genuinely something behind it to blur, over photos and video, because that concession is what produced the present state: it is the same visual idiom, so it keeps re-seeding the pattern everywhere else, and a chip on an arbitrary recipe photo is read more reliably as an opaque object than as a tinted window onto the picture. The landing page, which is the look this is converging on, uses no blur at all.

## Consequences

- `cssGlassBackdrop`, `cssGlassBackdropChip`, `cssGlassBackdropPanel` and `cssGlassBackdropShadow` are deleted from `apps/web/config/css-tokens.ts`. Their absence is the enforcement: there is no shared token left to reach for, so re-adding glass means writing it out by hand.
- Things sitting on photos carry their own contrast — opaque surface fill plus a shadow — instead of white text over a tinted scrim. Allergy chips keep their warning fill, which was already doing the work with colour rather than with blur.
- Lightbox, video player and carousel controls become solid near-black. They sit over real media, so they are exactly the case the rejected concession would have protected, and they are the clearest illustration of why it was rejected: a blurred scrim over a moving picture is the least stable surface to put a control on.
- This is a web decision. `apps/mobile` keeps `glass-back-button.tsx` and its platform blur, because on native that is a real compositor effect with real material semantics and the reasoning above does not transfer.
- Translucency is not banned as a technique — modal backdrops still dim the page, which is a scrim over content and not a pretend material. What is banned is a surface pretending to be glass.
