# Nothing about the Pantry is stored on the recipe or the grocery

A household wants a recipe's staples — olive oil, salt, flour — left off the grocery list, because it always has them. The obvious shapes are a flag on the recipe ingredient ("we have this"), a flag on the grocery ("do not buy"), or a fuzzy match of ingredient words against a list of staples. We chose none of them: a Pantry Ingredient is a household fact — one Ingredient the household keeps, added by a member and read across the household exactly as a Store is — and whether a line is _in the pantry_ is worked out when it is read, by ADR-0037's rule: a Pantry Ingredient of the same Ingredient, or of a kind of it, never the reverse. Nothing is stored on the recipe ingredient and nothing on the grocery row.

The Pantry is consulted where recipes are chosen and shopped for: when a recipe is added to the groceries, and by the Library filter that orders recipes by the share of their lines in the pantry. When a recipe is added, its stocked lines are shown apart, unticked, and left off the list; a tick is the only way one gets on anyway, and that tick is for this adding and nothing more. A grocery that is on the list is on it: the Pantry never removes, hides or greys a line, and a name typed by hand into the list is added whether or not it is in the pantry, because a person who types "salt" wants salt.

Running out is not taking a food out. A kept food is put on the groceries and stays in the Pantry, and while a Grocery of that same Ingredient is still to buy it is _on the list_; ticking that Grocery off is the restock. _On the list_ deliberately differs from _in the pantry_: only the same Ingredient counts, never a kind of it, because red onions bought for one recipe restock nobody's onions, and a ticked Grocery never counts. It too is derived from the household's groceries when read, so nothing is stored for it. Taking a food out of the Pantry means the household does not keep it.

The Pantry travels on its own router and its own subscription rather than on the Store's: it belongs to no Store and is read where no Store is on screen, on the recipe page. There is no REST endpoint; it is tRPC alone, like product linking and aisle filing.

## Consequences

- A Pantry Ingredient points at the Ingredient Alias the member's text resolved to and keeps that alias's Ingredient beside it (ADR-0037); its name is the food's. Deleting an Ingredient takes the Pantry Ingredients pointing at it.
- One Ingredient appears once per member, held by a row constraint. The household-wide rule, one Ingredient once across the household, is held in the repository, which answers an add with the item the household already holds rather than writing a second. The check and the write are one transaction under a lock on the Ingredient, so two members adding the same food at the same moment get one row between them, the second told about the first.
- _In the pantry_ is `pantryIngredientFor` and _on the list_ is `groceryOnTheList`, both in `@norish/shared/lib/pantry`; the add-to-groceries panel, the Pantry page and the Ingredient panel ask them, and the Library filter's query follows the same rule. No other rule may be added beside them. A text nothing has resolved yet is matched on the resolver's spelling keys, and on nothing looser.
- The add-to-groceries panel derives the split when it renders, so a Pantry Ingredient added by a housemate reshapes the panel at once.
- Another household's recipe is in the pantry by what each line's text resolves to in the reader's household (ADR-0042).
