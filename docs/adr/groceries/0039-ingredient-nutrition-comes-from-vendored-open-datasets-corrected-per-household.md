# Ingredient Nutrition comes from vendored open datasets, corrected per household

A recipe that supplies no Nutrition Information had none unless the language model estimated it, so an instance without AI never had any. The catalogue (ADR-0038) already names every food a recipe line resolves to, and the Open Food Facts taxonomy it is seeded from carries, on about a fifth of its entries, a code into CIQUAL or USDA: a food's calories, fat, carbohydrates and protein are one join away, with no name matching and no AI.

**The numbers come from CIQUAL 2025 (with 2020 for codes 2025 dropped), CALNUT, and USDA SR Legacy and Foundation, joined through the taxonomy's codes.** A name match against CIQUAL, USDA and CoFID names (exact, then normalised; never fuzzy) fills entries without a code. An Ingredient's own numbers are looked up in this order, first hit wins: the household's correction, Norish's fix list, its own CIQUAL code then proxy, CALNUT, its own USDA code, other CIQUAL-keyed codes, its name match. Only then does it borrow from its nearest Parent Ingredient with numbers of its own, along Norish's tree, so a parent a person chose lends too. Own before inherited is a deliberate break from Open Food Facts' own lookup, which takes an ancestor's code before an entry's own proxy and so serves white wine as pure alcohol and cinnamon as "spice (average)". Piece weights come from the taxonomy's `average_weight_per_unit`, then USDA's portion table; densities from Norish's density fixes, then USDA's cup and spoon portions, then the taxonomy's `density_g_per_ml`. Water is never assumed, and no spoon weighs the same whatever it holds.

**Norish keeps the source numbers as a committed table, rebuilt by a monthly workflow.** A script downloads the datasets, reduces them to source code → four numbers, piece weight and density, adds the name matches keyed by Open Food Facts id, and opens a pull request when the result changed, so a new match is read by a person before it lands. A failed run (ANSES moves its download links per edition) is retried the next month. An instance applies the table at boot when a release carries a new version of it; the nightly taxonomy seed still decides which code an entry has, so a code fixed upstream arrives overnight.

**Norish keeps three lists of its own in that table: a fix list, density fixes and lenders that never lend.** The taxonomy's codes are a contributor's choice and some are wrong for cooking: `en:milk` is skimmed milk, `en:corn` dry grain, `en:beef-broth` dehydrated stock, `en:pistachio-nuts` macadamia, and beef, pork, lamb and cumin have no code at all. The fix list points an entry at the dataset food it should use, never at typed numbers. A density fix does the same for the density alone (decided 2026-10-02): CIQUAL weighs no spoons, so an entry whose numbers come from CIQUAL takes the spoon weight USDA measured for the same food, and cumin reads CIQUAL's cumin and USDA's teaspoon of it. A density fix on a group whose foods share a form (spices, sauces, syrups, vinegars and creams; oils already carry the taxonomy's) gives the group a density its members borrow. Cream is the one dairy group: every poured or spooned cream weighs 0.96–1.01 g/ml, where dairy as a whole holds milk and grated cheese. The lenders that never lend (sauce, alcohol, fruit and the few others a leave-one-out test found wrong most of the time) cost about a hundred niche entries their borrowed numbers; their children's lines show as not counted rather than off by hundreds of calories. They stop only the numbers: an average sauce misleads about calories, not about what a spoonful weighs, so a density walks past them. None of the lists is sent upstream.

**A correction belongs to a household, not to the Ingredient.** Everything else about an Ingredient is instance-wide, but whether "milk" means skimmed is a household's habit, not a fact about milk. Any household member may correct any Ingredient, seeded or not, with no edit policy in the way: a correction names a different dataset food or gives label numbers per 100 g, and the same holds for piece weight and density. It is kept apart from the dataset's numbers, keyed by the member who made it and read by their household, the most recent winning, so a table refresh never touches it. A recipe's worked-out nutrition is therefore computed when it is read, for the reader's household, and never stored on the recipe.

## Considered and rejected

- **Each instance fetching the datasets itself**, as the taxonomy is fetched. About 20 MB from four hosts, for files that change every few years, and the name matches would land unread.
- **NEVO.** Its conditions reserve copyright, forbid amendment and forbid charging end users, so it cannot be offered under the ODbL.
- **The Canadian Nutrient File.** Every USDA code it would join through already resolves in SR Legacy, and OGL-Canada's compatibility with the ODbL is unsettled.
- **Product databases** (Open Food Facts' product export and its per-category averages). Gigabytes per instance for brand-exact numbers, which Ingredient Nutrition deliberately is not.
- **Fuzzy and head-noun name matching.** 57% and 75% precision on hand-checked samples.
- **Borrowing from the direct parent only.** Error depends on which parent lends, not how far away it is: blocking the worst lenders avoids more bad borrows for a fifth of the lost coverage.
- **One instance-wide correction**, an administrator's alone for seeded Ingredients. A household that buys skimmed milk would have to accept another's whole milk.
- **One weight per spoon for every food**, a tablespoon being 15 g of whatever it holds. A tablespoon of flour weighs 8 g, of grated parmesan 5 g and of honey 21 g.
- **Densities computed per group**, as the median of its foods' USDA densities. A group's foods need not share a form: USDA's dried leafy herbs weigh 0.12 g/ml against 0.43 for ground spices, and dairy holds both milk and grated cheese, so a cup of grated cheese would weigh 247 g instead of 113 g. A group lends a density only where a person picked it.
- **A density-only name match against USDA's names** ("Spices, cumin seed" is cumin). The foods missing a density are a few dozen much-used ones a person can list exactly, and the normalisation keeps USDA's category words on purpose.
- **A unit sized by region or language**: a 250 ml metric cup, a 200 ml Korean cup, a 20 ml Australian tablespoon. A line keeps its unit, not the word or language it was written in, and a recipe's language is deliberately not recorded; one size per unit is off by 4% for a metric cup.

## Consequences

- Amends ADR-0038, whose rejection of CIQUAL and USDA held only while macros were out of scope; the catalogue seed now keeps each entry's nutrition codes, piece weight and density instead of discarding its properties.
- Credit: the Ingredients page names CIQUAL 2025 (ANSES), CALNUT, USDA FoodData Central and CoFID beside Open Food Facts, and a worked-out total names the sources it used. A household's correction is its own data, not the dataset's, which keeps CIQUAL's "not altered" condition.
- A line with no amount, or none that reaches grams, is left out of the total, and the total names every line it left out and why, a pinch, a dash or "to taste" included (decided 2026-10-02; until then those counted as nothing, unnamed). A total says it is estimated when the lines that borrowed something bring at least a tenth of its calories, so a borrowed teaspoon of paprika no longer marks every total.
- On an instance with AI, the language model estimates the lines a worked-out total leaves out, seasoning excepted, given the counted lines as facts, one per-serving share per line. The shares are stored apart from the recipe's Nutrition Information under each line's key (its food or text, amount and unit), and a reader's total adds the share of each line still left out for them: a line a household's own correction counts simply counts, the other lines keep the model's numbers, and a line edited since is listed as not counted until the next estimate (decided 2026-10-01, ticket 12; per line since the review of the same day, so one correction never drops the whole estimate).
- A piece weight is borrowed like the numbers, except by an entry on Norish's piece fixes (decided 2026-10-02): the dataset food whose piece it weighs, or none for a zest. A kind's size is what tells it from its parent; an egg yolk borrowing an egg's 60 g counted three yolks' fat for one. A line nothing else weighs takes the weight its brackets state, and a size word stored as its unit counts one piece.
- Legacy lines reach seeded Ingredients through the resolver changes in ADR-0037's amendment: units-map phrases stripped at rung 2, a flagged mint parented under the seeded alias its text ends with, and a pass that re-resolves old flagged mints whenever the rungs change, skipping any a person kept distinct.
