# Line Cost is pack arithmetic over what the shop presents

A grocery row carries its own amount ("700 g flour", "2 cola", "12 eggs") and a Store Product carries what one Shelf Price buys, and the two have to meet in one number for the list to add up. We chose the arithmetic a till does and nothing cleverer: a Line Cost is as many whole packs as the amount needs, rounded up strictly, at the Shelf Price; a bare number is a number of packs unless the shop itself counts the pack in pieces, in which case it is a number of pieces; and what is sold loose is priced by weight, proportionally, because rounding 300 g of bananas up to a kilo is exactly the wrong pricing the feature exists to fix. That last rule amends ADR-0028, which rejected every per-unit price: a per-unit price that is a product's only price is its Shelf Price, and the per-kilo comparison printed beside a pack price is still not. A Sale is, in the same spirit, only what the shop presents: a price with the regular price it replaces beside it, plus the shop's own words for the deal. Dirk shows its offers that way and Albert Heijn shows a deal as a label over its regular price, so the one rule prices Dirk's markdowns and shows AH's "2 voor €5.50" as words, which is what a shopper wants from a deal that needs a card. We rejected a tolerance on rounding (a "ca. 405 g" piece is still a piece you cannot buy 1.02 of, and one rule with no number in it has nothing to re-guess), deal arithmetic across packs (a second feature, and the words already inform), a vocabulary of card and membership words per shop (the list the search-address design refused to keep, wrong in every language it has not reached), and a per-line "packs needed" override (the amount and the Pack Size are the two knobs, and a third would let them disagree).

## Consequences

- The store total is the sum of the Line Costs still to buy, so the two list views can disagree: two flat rows of 200 g flour are two packs, the grouped row they make is one. Each view's heading is the sum of the rows under it, which is the promise that matters.
- Reconciling amounts needs a table of units with families, magnitudes and spellings, the one deliberate word list in this area. Units are a closed set, unlike search words, and the table lives in code rather than admin config so nobody can misconfigure it.
- A line the arithmetic cannot do (a measure against a count, a unit the table does not know, a product with no Pack Size, or more than 24 packs) costs one pack and says so, rather than nothing: a linked product priced at nothing reads as a bug.
- A hand-set Pack Size is the last word and no reading replaces it, the same rule as a hand-typed price. When a shop reformulates a pack, the correction goes stale until its owner touches it; accepted, since the alternative is a reading overwriting a human.
- A Sale learned from a results card outlives a refresh that reads the product page at the same price, because that page may not restate the regular price; a refresh that reads any other price ends it. Silence about the regular price is not evidence the deal ended; a changed price is.
- Card-gated prices cannot be told from markdowns by markup. A shop that presents a card price as its main price will be priced at it, and Norish cannot know better.
