# Ingredient identity is an alias pointing at an Ingredient

Keyed by text, a household's ingredients are whatever its recipes hold: "onion", "onions", "onion, diced" and "ui" are four rows, and everything a household teaches Norish about a food — a Product Link, an Aisle Link, a store preference, a Pantry Ingredient — is taught once per spelling. A tidier list alone changes nothing while every reader matches on text.

**An Ingredient is one food, known by many Ingredient Aliases, and every reference points at an alias.** A recipe line, a Grocery and a recurring grocery keep their text exactly as written ("onions, diced" still reads that way, so a shopper can still choose to buy them pre-diced) and store the alias that text resolved to; the alias points at the Ingredient. A Pantry Ingredient stores its alias too, but is about the food rather than a line, so it shows the Ingredient. An alias's fold — the one grocery folding — is unique within a household's catalogue, the shared seed plus the household's own foods, spellings and junk marks, so one spelling means one food to a household; resolution, merges and AI never cross households (ADR-0042). Product Links, Aisle Links and store preferences are facts about an Ingredient at a Store, so teaching Norish where "onion" is filed files every alias of it. A merge moves aliases from one Ingredient to another and deletes the emptied one; nothing that points at an alias moves, which is what makes a wrong merge one alias move to undo.

**A spelling is a clean name.** A text with preparation, brackets or an amount ("gyoza vellen ((ontdooid))", "12 gyoza vellen") points at the clean spelling it reduces to and is never one of its own, and a spelling a person types is stored clean, so a recipe's text can match it. The seed's spellings are the exception, since a bracket there tells two foods apart ("paprika (vrucht)", "aroma (mela)"): they keep it, and only stray marks are cleaned ("3wu)" is "3wu"). Letting every new text become a spelling was rejected: on a production copy it made 71,609 spellings no recipe wrote, "gyoza vellen )" among them.

**One resolver mints, and every caller goes through it.** Recipe save and import, pantry add, and grocery and recurring-grocery create and rename hand their text to the resolver and store the alias it answers with; the parser keeps emitting names without minting. The resolver reads by the words alone and asks AI nothing, so nothing that writes a food waits on a model. It cleans the text of stray punctuation (unmatched and doubled brackets, a leading "+ / - :", prices, notes), then tries an exact alias match on the fold (rung 1), then the fold with what is not the food stripped, or a plural or diminutive of a known spelling (rung 2), then the words before a note, then a slip of the fingers, and only then mints a new Ingredient under its clean spelling. The words it reads by are the Ingredient Words.

The resolver lives in `@norish/shared-server/ingredients`, not in `@norish/api`: the tRPC routers, the queue workers and the archive importer all write recipes, and none of them may import `@norish/api`. The repository layer below it cannot mint: a recipe write takes the resolver's answers alongside its payload (`WithIngredientResolutions`), so a path that skipped the resolver does not typecheck.

The trade-off is deliberate: the words never guess. They find a food by its spelling, by what rung 2 strips, by a plural, by the words before a note or by one slip, never by a word it contains, so "salt" never becomes "salted butter". A new Ingredient minted where the words were unsure is cheaper to merge later than a wrong match is to notice, because a wrong match silently lends one food's Product Link and Aisle to another.

**AI looks at a minted food afterwards, only where an administrator lets it, and its answer is applied.** Each automatic action is an administrator's opt-in under Admin → AI processing → Ingredient catalogue (`AI_CONFIG.automaticIngredients`: `cleanUp`, `nutrition`, `conversions`, `icon`, all off), following the AI switch. With `cleanUp` on, AI's review asks about a food moments after someone's action mints it; otherwise AI asks only when an administrator starts a Catalogue Cleanup. Seeded foods are never asked about. Every answer is acted on, sure or not, and each change is an AI Change: listed for a month beside the Ingredients with an Undo that reverses that one change and is remembered, so AI never makes it again. A sure answer raises no flag; an unsure one is applied and flags its food, so a flag says only that AI changed a food without being sure. A food AI has not answered for (reviewing off, AI off, failing, or its key refused) is unreviewed rather than flagged. Asking a person to confirm each answer was rejected: on a production copy, 129 of 432 own Ingredients had been marked distinct by hand (toilet paper, floor cleaner, "deegroller"), each a flag nobody needed.

## Consequences

- Ingredients and aliases belong to a household, none for the seed, and record the member who added them; an Ingredient carries a flag, an alias an optional locale and a seeded marker. Any member edits the household's own foods; the seed is the administrator's, and a spelling a household adds to a seeded food is the household's (ADR-0042).
- A recipe line points at its spellings alone, with no food beside them: a single pointer cannot disagree with itself, and a merge's Undo needs to know which lines wrote which spelling. A Grocery, a recurring grocery and a Pantry Ingredient keep their alias's Ingredient beside the alias, so every read of the list has the food its price, aisle and store preference are keyed by without a join; the code that writes an alias writes its Ingredient, and a merge re-points both. The client looks prices and aisles up by the grocery's Ingredient; a line added offline has none until the server resolves it, and is unpriced and unfiled until then. A store preference falls back to a fuzzy match over the names of the Ingredients preferred.
- A recipe line may point at several aliases, needed together ("zout en peper") or as alternatives ("boter of olie"), and keeps its text. A Grocery and a Pantry Ingredient point at one, and a text naming several becomes one per food, so every fact kept per food (a Product Link, an Aisle Link, the pantry check) stays one row to one food; letting them hold several would make every reader of a list keyed by food choose between them. A line of alternatives is in the pantry when any of them is kept, puts its first on the list otherwise and counts as its first in nutrition; a line of foods needed together gives each its full amount; the line shows its first food's icon.
- A name is split only where it is not known whole ("mac and cheese" is one) and every part is a known food, at an "and" or an "or" of any language. "&" and "+" join foods needed together and "/" alternatives, also between two letters ("salt/pepper"), never inside a joining word written with one ("and/or", "en/of"); "of" joins alternatives only where the parts are not all English ("juice of a lemon" is one). The joining words split the plain name, so "pinch of salt and pepper" is salt and pepper. A comma list's first piece is the line's food as rung 2 reads it, known or minted, and the later pieces that are known foods join it, each also split at its "and" words; the rest is preparation. Only the whole text as written, known, is no list. AI splits what the words cannot, only when sure.
- Rung 2 strips, at either end of a name, a vague amount or serving phrase from the units map ("salt to taste", "a pinch of nutmeg", "for garnish", "optional"), a size, and a preparation word ("crushed", "finely chopped", "peeled and", "gesnipperde"; never "ground", "dried" or "smoked", which name a different food); and at its start a container ("can of chickpeas", "pak koriander"), a weight or a volume with or without a number before it, a quantity an import left there ("ongeveer 4 el bosui"), a connector ("di farina" is farina, "d'aglio" aglio; one inside a name stays, "farina di semola"), and the "null" an old import wrote for a missing amount ("null zout"). A piece is never stripped, since those words name foods too ("glass noodles", "filet de poulet"). Which units are weights and volumes is the unit table's (`MEASURE_MAP_IDS` in `@norish/shared/lib/units`), the one nutrition reads a line's unit by; the spellings stripped are the units map's. Four seeded spellings start with a measure ("gram flour", "gram-meel", "grammo nero", "cup mushroom"): they match exactly, and only a variant of them is read without it. Rung 2 also reads a plural or diminutive of a known spelling as its food, by the words' endings, both ways ("bosuien" and "bosuitjes" are "bosui"; "krieltje" finds "krieltjes"). A `#` heading, an amount alone ("el", "200 g"), a line with no letter or digit, and the "[object Object]" an old import wrote name no food.
- The words rung 2 and the parent from the words read by — preparation, sizes, cuts and forms (`forms`), approximations, connectors, the joining words, the words that open a note (`endsBefore`), and the plural and diminutive endings — are data per language beside the units map (`ingredient-words.default.json`, server config `ingredient_words`), shipped for every language Norish speaks and read together, since a recipe's language is not recorded. They are the administrator's to edit under Content Detection, and an edited set stops following releases. Words that go both ways stay out ("flakes", "leaves").
- A text not known whole is read by its words before a note ("for", "voor", "naar", "uit", "om") where those are a known food and the words after it name none, so "greek yogurt for topping" is greek yogurt, "olie om te bakken" is olie and "kip met rijst" stays whole. A note word that also joins foods somewhere (Spanish "en") is no note, and junk before a note names no food ("Bakvorm met opstaande randen"). This reading only finds a food: it never names a mint, and the recheck merges nothing by it.
- A text no rung knows that is one slip of the fingers from one known food's spelling finds that food: one letter wrong, left out or added, or two neighbours swapped, never the first letter, from five letters up, and only where one food is that near (`@norish/shared/lib/name-slips`, the grocery product rule's, ADR-0028). "parmezan" is "parmezaan"; "visfilet" is not "kipfilet" and "wraps" is not Danish "raps", since two letters apart, or the first letter, named another food as often as a typo. The slip is taken at once, AI on or off, and the text joins the food under its clean spelling, which a person moves where it was wrong.
- The catalogue keeps a junk pile of folds that name no food (`ingredient_junk_pile`): the seed's list of generic words in every catalogue language (`JUNK_SEED`, applied at boot once per version, never re-adding an entry a person took off) plus each household's own marks. Where the resolver cuts a name into pieces, at a comma or at its joining words, a piece whose fold or plain fold is on the pile is dropped and the name is read on the rest; a name all on the pile names no food. AI's not-a-food deletion puts its spellings there with its AI Change, whose Undo takes them off whoever put them there; a person removing a spelling by hand may say it is not a food; a person adding the spelling takes it off. The pile is read with the resolver's cached rules, so another process sees a change within `RULES_TTL_MS`.
- A mint is named for its Plain Name by the Ingredient Words, and is not flagged. It takes a parent from its words where a known spelling ends its name ("ground cumin" under cumin) or starts it before cuts, forms and sizes alone ("mango blokjes" under mango), filed without a question; a spelling anywhere else in the name gives no parent, and a one-word cut is never a parent window ("peen julienne" is not under ling, which French spells "julienne"). The window is looked up among seeded spellings first, then among the household's listed own foods, never the mint itself, so "roodschillige krieltjes" goes under the own "krieltjes". A parent from the words sets a parent, never a merge. A mint that is written files the undecided parentless own foods minted before it whose words find it, so the order of an import does not matter.
- A mint that loses a race to any of its spellings is removed again, and the spellings it did get join the Ingredient that won; a name already held by an Ingredient, in any case, joins it rather than minting a second.
- Readers never mint. A grocery panel asking what a Store remembers for a name being typed asks `ingredients.find`, which answers with what rungs 1 and 2 find, or nothing.
- Unresolved text is matched on the resolver's first two rungs on the client too. The fold and the preparation stripping live in one module, `@norish/shared/lib/spelling-keys`, that the resolver and the clients share; a text nothing has resolved yet (typed on the screen, or added offline) is matched on its food key, the stripped fold where anything is left and else the fold, so "onions, diced" typed offline is covered by a Pantry "onions" before it syncs, as it will be after. A grocery added offline is grouped by that key for filing, which makes "onions, diced" and "onions" one group: the one Ingredient they will resolve to.
- An Ingredient may have a Parent Ingredient, the more general food it is a kind of ("red onion" under "onion"), set on the Ingredients page, by AI's review or from the words. A child with no Aisle Link at a Store is filed in its nearest ancestor's Aisle. A child in the Pantry covers a recipe line for any ancestor, never the reverse: having the specific food counts as having the general one, but a recipe that asked for the specific food still gets it on the list. A food with no Ingredient Icon of its own shows its nearest ancestor's, and broad groups at the top of the tree are left without one, so the borrowing stops before it turns vague. Ingredient Nutrition borrows along the tree too (ADR-0039). Product Links, store preferences and grocery grouping ignore it, so the generic food is never bought for a recipe that asked for the specific one. A parent that would close a cycle is refused, under one lock on the whole tree; a merge re-parents the source's children onto the target, the target first taking the source's place where it sat below it.
- Whenever the rungs change (`RUNG_VERSION`), the undecided mints, own foods no person kept distinct, renamed or parented, are read again, oldest first: one whose spellings name one other food under the new rules merges into it; one naming several is left; any other takes today's plain name, as a spelling and as its name where free, and its words' parent where neither AI nor a person gave it one. The recheck, like the upgrade and the seed, never asks AI.
- A recipe line with an amount and no unit is read for its food as the recipe editor reads it, number in front (`lineFoodText` in `@norish/shared/lib/ingredient-text`), on every save and in the upgrade: there the parser takes a unit of any kind ("1 | – | rol bladerdeeg" is bladerdeeg, "0.25 | – | krop ijsbergsla" iceberg lettuce). Never without a number, since the parser takes a leading unit word even then ("glass noodles" would be noodles by the glass). The line keeps its text, amount and unit. A recipe save keeps the food of every line whose text did not change, so a text only AI could connect is not asked about again.
- What still names nothing after the rungs and AI is a Junk Line: it stays in its recipe as written and points at no food, so the cook can fix it, and only a food it minted leaves the catalogue. Keeping the catalogue never changes a recipe's lines. A line naming a tool stays and names no food. A colon line with no amount that an older import left names no food and keeps its text; it is not turned into a Heading. A Grocery or Pantry Ingredient a person typed is never deleted, and points at no food where it names none.
- Deleting an Ingredient or a spelling something uses is allowed. Each recipe line, Grocery and recurring grocery that named a deleted Ingredient is resolved again from its own text, AI off and minting nothing: with the deleted food out of the way the joining words can split it, so "zout en peper" under a deleted "kruidenmix" names zout and peper, a Grocery of it becomes one per food as when typed, a text another food knows whole names that food, and anything else ("kruidenmix") names none, so a delete never mints its food back. Its Pantry Ingredients go, since they have no text. A removed spelling's lines, Groceries and Pantry Ingredients move to another spelling of the same food, the one that is its name, else the oldest, so nothing loses its food and nothing moves to a parent; a wrong translation is fixed with a move, which carries its lines.
- An own Ingredient someone's action minted is deleted once no recipe line, Grocery, recurring grocery or Pantry Ingredient has named it for three months and no food is a kind of it; a seeded one never is, nor one a person added by hand. Recurring groceries offer "yearly" so a yearly purchase keeps its food. The same nightly sweep drops AI Changes older than a month.
- AI's review asks one question per food and sends no catalogue. AI says what the name is: one food (its plain English name; `parents`, two or three English wordings of the more general food it is a kind of, most specific first; `nativeParents`, the same in the name's language; a broad category; and other names a shop sells it under), several foods with their parts, or not a food, and whether it is sure. The code looks those names up; AI never finds the food itself. One food found is the merge; none, and a parent found, is the parent (the native wordings first, then the English, in AI's order, else the category among seeded foods); neither is a food of its own, which the Plain Name step follows. Only where the names find more than one food is a second question asked, over just those: the Decision where its _ingredient resolution_ use is on, else the language model, which answers with a listed food's name (a per-request enum of the candidates' names, or null). The reading and the second question are two Prompts, `ingredient-resolution` and `ingredient-match`, with each field's meaning in the answer schema's descriptions. The prompt tells AI the catalogue follows Open Food Facts' ingredient names and asks for an English name that keeps what makes a food another product ("baby potatoes").
- AI's names are looked up as written: by their whole spelling, its fold, the fold without what follows a comma or sits in brackets, their plurals, and a food's own name; never with sizes or preparation words stripped, so "petite potatoes" does not find potato, and a narrower food AI names whole stays its own food, filed under AI's parent. A slip is measured from the whole spelling, and the foods AI is shown at the second question are a slip apart. AI's names and a mint's parent from the words find a seeded food only by a spelling in a language Norish reads (`CATALOGUE_LANGUAGES`) or in every language, so burger buns never find pear through Volapük "bün"; a person's spellings always count, and so does a food's own name. Rungs 1 and 2 read every language, so a Dutch recipe's "paprika" still finds bell pepper. Where an Open Food Facts entry's languages share a spelling, the seed files it under the one Norish reads ("kaas" is Dutch, not Afrikaans), and each applied file re-stamps a kept spelling's language (`SEED_CORRECTIONS_VERSION`).
- Where AI's other names or a slip find the same food as one of AI's parents, the name is filed under that parent as a food of its own, so "italian sausage" stays its own food under sausage although sausage holds "salsiccia". Where AI's English name finds the food by its own spelling, the name is that food and merges into it, parent or not ("edamame beans" into edamame). An other name that is one of AI's parents by spelling, plurals and diminutives of every language included (`sameFood`, read whole), also says the name is that food, so "guajillo peppers" with the other name "guajillo chiles" merges into guajillo chili; "sliced ham" is not ham, and "broccolini", a slip of broccoli, is not broccoli. A parent AI gives that is the last word or words of the name of the parent the food has keeps that parent and is not looked up, so "red pointed peppers" stays under red bell pepper when AI says "pepper". Where AI names a parent or a category and the lookup finds neither, the food keeps a parent an AI Change gave it that is still listed and not undone (`wasParentSetByAI`); any other parent, one from the words included, is dropped, as is any parent when the reading names neither. A person's parent is never asked about.
- A sure answer is the Decision's threshold or the language model's `sure`. A food only a slip finds, picked at the second question, is sure where the Decision chose it with probability at least `SURE_SLIP_THRESHOLD` (0.9) or the language model said sure. One rule holds for a merge, a split part, a parent and a category.
- A tool (the reading's reason `equipment`) is deleted, sure or not. A sure "not a food" for anything else deletes the food and puts its spellings on the junk pile; an unsure one flags the food `ai-junk` and deletes nothing. A food a Pantry Ingredient names stays, flagged. A deleted food's rows are resolved again as any delete's are, and its Undo brings the food back to them.
- AI's review gives an own food its Plain Name, reading what the Ingredient Words missed and taking off what a food comes in or is served with ("GR TONIJN UIT BLIK" is "tonijn", so canned and fresh share one food). A rename is never flagged. A name differing from AI's only in case takes AI's casing; one differing only in accents or punctuation is settled as it is. A plain name another listed Ingredient already spells, in a language Norish reads, is that food, so the two merge. A name AI gave, or a person gave, took or undid, is settled: AI gives no other, and no later reading of the words renames it.
- A Catalogue Cleanup over recipe foods asks about the own foods recipes brought in that no person decided about. Over the whole catalogue it asks about every listed own food, overriding what a person decided and what an Undo said for that run alone, and asks nothing about a seeded food, only checking the spellings people, imports and merges added to one: the seed is always right.
- With `icon` on, a new food's icon is drawn after AI's review, or straight after the mint where `cleanUp` is off, for a food that still exists and still shows none. `nutrition` and `conversions` let AI fill what the datasets lack (Fill Gaps). Every AI action the catalogue offers is gated like every other: hidden with AI off, and refused by the procedures.
- AI's review is an ingredients feature that happens to use AI, as recipe extraction is an import feature: the reading and the match live with the AI code (`ai/resolution/ingredient-resolution.ts`), called by the review (`ingredients/review.ts`), and reach a model only through the AI runtime.
- The upgrade from before 0.25 is expand, migrate, contract. At startup, after the first seed (ADR-0038), every recipe line, Grocery, recurring grocery and Pantry Ingredient that names no food is resolved from its text by the resolver with AI off, as a new import would be; the old `ingredients` rows are no Ingredients of their own. Carrying every old row over as an Ingredient was rejected: on a production copy it left 2,189 line texts as foods of their own, and the old rows' spellings took the seed's most common words. The fold is the JavaScript folding, so this cannot be SQL. Recipe lines hold their text as written in a column of their own, since the Ingredient's name is not what they display. Product Links, Aisle Links and store preferences learned by folded name are parked by the migration and land on the Ingredient the household's groceries of that name resolved to, else on the one the name itself resolves to (minted where need be); two that land on one Ingredient at one Store keep the most recently updated, and one whose Store went is dropped.
- The first-start cleanup brings an older catalogue to the current reading, once per `CLEANUP_VERSION`, with AI off and never changing a seeded food or its spellings beyond their stray marks. The junk pile is seeded first. Every variant spelling's rows move to its clean spelling and the variant goes; a joined own food is split and removed, even one a person kept distinct; an own food whose spelling reads, up to its note, as another known food merges into it, kept distinct or named, unless a person chose its parent. Its last step reads every recipe line that names a food again from its text, matching the spellings people and AI placed first so their decisions hold, and writes only a line that reads other foods under today's rules; a line whose reading names none loses an own food and keeps a seeded one. An own food the lines named before the re-read, or whose every spelling is on the junk pile, is deleted where nothing names it and it is no one's parent; other unused own foods wait for the nightly sweep. A hidden food carries no flag, a flag whose reason is gone is lifted (keeping `ai-unsure` where an unsure change is still open), and a parent 0.25 marked chosen from a confirmed suggestion counts as no person's choice.
