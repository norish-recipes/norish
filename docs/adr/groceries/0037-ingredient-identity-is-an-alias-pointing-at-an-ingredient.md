# Ingredient identity is an alias pointing at an Ingredient

Norish's ingredients were a flat list of whatever text a recipe once held: "onion", "onions", "onion, diced" and "ui" were four rows, and everything a household taught Norish about a food — a Product Link, an Aisle Link, a store preference, a Pantry Ingredient — was keyed by folded name text and so taught once per spelling. A tidier list alone would change nothing, because every reader matched on text.

**An Ingredient is one food, known by many Ingredient Aliases, and every reference points at an alias.** A recipe line, a Grocery and a recurring grocery keep their text exactly as written ("onions, diced" still reads that way, so a shopper can still choose to buy them pre-diced) and store the alias that text resolved to; the alias points at the Ingredient. A Pantry Ingredient stores its alias too, but is about the food rather than a line, so it shows the Ingredient. An alias's fold — the one grocery folding — is unique, so one spelling means one food instance-wide. Product Links, Aisle Links and store preferences are facts about an Ingredient at a Store, so teaching Norish where "onion" is filed files every alias of it. A merge moves aliases from one Ingredient to another and deletes the emptied one; nothing that points at an alias moves, which is what makes a wrong merge one alias move to undo.

**One resolver mints, and every caller goes through it.** Recipe save and import, pantry add, and grocery and recurring-grocery create and rename hand their text to the resolver and store the alias it answers with; the parser keeps emitting names without minting. The order is an exact alias match on the fold, then the fold with the preparation stripped (the text after the first comma, and anything bracketed), then a Decision or the language model, and only then a new Ingredient with the text as its first alias. A mint that nothing sure vouched for is a Flagged Ingredient, for a person to merge or mark distinct. Without AI the middle rung is skipped and resolution still matches exact and stripped names.

The resolver lives in `@norish/shared-server/ingredients`, not in `@norish/api`: the tRPC routers, the queue workers and the archive importer all write recipes, and none of them may import `@norish/api`. The repository layer below it cannot mint: a recipe write takes the resolver's answers alongside its payload (`WithIngredientResolutions`), so a path that skipped the resolver does not typecheck.

The trade-off is deliberate: the resolver never guesses from words on its own. "onions" and "onion" are one food only once the seed, a Decision or a person says so, and "salt" never becomes "salted butter". A new Ingredient minted where Norish was unsure is cheaper to merge later than a wrong match is to notice, because a wrong match silently lends one food's Product Link and Aisle to another.

## Consequences

- Supersedes ADR-0036's "a Pantry Ingredient is a name matched exactly": the Pantry matches on Ingredient. Amends ADR-0031's "an aisle is a fact about a name": it is a fact about an Ingredient at a Store.
- The migration is expand, migrate, contract. Existing Ingredients gain their own name as an alias at startup, oldest first, so where two names fold alike the older Ingredient keeps the spelling; the fold is the JavaScript folding, so this cannot be SQL. Recipe lines gain their text as written in a column of their own, because the Ingredient's name is no longer what they display. The contract drops the recipe line's direct Ingredient pointer (a line reaches its food only through its alias) and the Ingredient's folded name. The Product Link, Aisle Link and store preference tables keep their old folded name one release longer, read by the upgrade alone: the upgrade runs at startup, after every migration, so a migration that dropped it would drop what an old link was about.
- Ingredients carry an owner (whoever's action minted it; none for the seed) and a flag; aliases carry an owner, an optional locale and a seeded marker. The edit policy and the Ingredients page read these.
- A mint that loses a race to any of its spellings is removed again, and the spellings it did get join the Ingredient that won; a name already held by an Ingredient, in any case, joins it rather than minting a second.
- A Grocery and a recurring grocery keep their alias's Ingredient beside the alias, as a recipe line and a Pantry Ingredient do, so every read of the list has the food its price, aisle and store preference are keyed by without a join; the code that writes an alias writes its Ingredient, and a merge re-points both. The client looks prices and aisles up by the grocery's Ingredient; a line added offline has none until the server resolves it, and is unpriced and unfiled until then.
- Rung 3 is an ingredients feature that happens to use AI, as recipe extraction is an import feature: it lives with the AI code (`ai/resolution/ingredient-resolution.ts`), the resolver calling it, and reaches a model only through the AI runtime, a Decision under the _ingredient resolution_ use or a request under the `ingredient-resolution` Prompt. The upgrade resolves without it.
- Readers never mint. A grocery panel asking what a Store remembers for a name being typed asks `ingredients.find`, which answers with an exact or stripped alias match or nothing.
- Links carried over from folded names land on the Ingredient the groceries of that name resolved to, else on the one the name itself resolves to (minted where need be, so no link is dropped); two that land on one Ingredient at one Store keep the most recently updated. A store preference keeps its fuzzy fallback, now over the names of the Ingredients preferred.
- An Ingredient may have a Parent Ingredient, the more general food it is a kind of ("red onion" under "onion"), set on the Ingredients page or by rung 3's "a kind of" answer. The tree reaches two readers and no more: a child with no Aisle Link at a Store is filed in its nearest ancestor's Aisle, and a child in the Pantry covers a recipe line for any ancestor, never the reverse — having the specific food counts as having the general one, but a recipe that asked for the specific food still gets it on the list. Product Links, store preferences and grocery grouping ignore the tree, so the generic food is never bought for a recipe that asked for the specific one. A parent that would close a cycle is refused, under one lock on the whole tree; a merge re-parents the source's children onto the target, the target first taking the source's place where it sat below it.
- Unresolved text is matched on the resolver's first two rungs on the client too (2026-09-30). The fold and the preparation stripping live in one module, `@norish/shared/lib/spelling-keys`, that the resolver and the clients share; a text nothing has resolved yet (typed on the screen, or added offline) is matched on its food key, the stripped fold where anything is left and else the fold, so "onions, diced" typed offline is covered by a Pantry "onions" before it syncs, as it will be after. A grocery added offline is grouped by that key for filing, which makes "onions, diced" and "onions" one group: the one Ingredient they will resolve to.
- Amended by ADR-0039 (2026-10-01), so lines written before the catalogue reach seeded Ingredients without AI. Rung 2 also strips a phrase from the units map at either end of the text ("salt to taste", "a pinch of nutmeg"), plus serving phrases added to it ("to serve", "for garnish", "optional"). Only its vague amounts and serving phrases are stripped, never a measure, piece or container, which name foods too ("onion rings" are not onion). A mint is given the seeded alias its text ends with, longest first, as its Parent Ingredient ("ground cumin" under cumin), and stays flagged: a guess from words may set a parent, never merge. Marking an Ingredient distinct is now recorded, and a pass re-resolves flagged Ingredients with no Open Food Facts id whenever the rungs change, skipping any a person kept distinct, renamed or parented.
