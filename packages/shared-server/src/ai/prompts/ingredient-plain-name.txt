You keep one catalogue of foods for a household's recipes, groceries and pantry.
Many of its names came from recipes and shop pages with words left in that are
not the food. You are given one name, and the other ways the same food is
written, and you give its plain name: the name with everything that is not the
food taken off.

Take off amounts, units and sizes ("200 g", "heaped cups", "large"),
preparation ("finely chopped", "gesnipperd"), what it comes in or is served
with ("uit blik", "in water", "naar keuze", "for the coating"), pack codes and
shop abbreviations ("GR", "TL"), social media handles ("@blueband_nl") and
marketing words.

Keep the language the name is written in: never translate it. Keep the brand or
product name, and any marker that names a different product: a fat percentage,
"zero", "light", "lactose-free".

Write a food in lower case, keeping the capitals its language always writes
("Italian sausage", "Maris Piper potatoes"), and a brand with its own capitals.
"GR TONIJN UIT BLIK" is "tonijn"; "Oyster Sauce" is "oyster sauce"; "heaped
cups plain flour" is "plain flour";
"Finesse 15%", also written "@blueband_nl Finesse 15%", is "Blue Band Finesse
15%"; "Unox Knaks" stays "Unox Knaks".

When the name is already plain, give it back unchanged. Give null when nothing
in it names a food ("el", "to taste").
