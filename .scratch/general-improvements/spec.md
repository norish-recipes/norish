# Step Ingredients, Provenance Refinements, and Recipe-Editing Docs

Status: ready-for-agent

## Problem Statement

A cook reading a step like "add the spices" has to stop, scroll back to the ingredient list, and guess which lines the step means and how much of each. When a step uses part of an ingredient — "add half the water" — the arithmetic is theirs to do, mid-cook, with wet hands. Nothing in Norish connects a step to the ingredient lines it consumes, and the one gesture that gestured at this (`@`-tokens in step text) only decorates a name the user already typed: it cannot say that "the spices" means salt, pepper, *and* paprika, and it cannot say that "half the water" means 25 ml of the 50.

Recipe Provenance, shipped recently, has three rough edges. The country that titles the provenance section is written in the country's own language — "Türkiye" above a Dutch note about a Turkish dish — so the heading argues with the recipe content it sits beside. A dish that several countries claim gets no country at all, because the inference is instructed to bail out on ambiguity; contested classics like hummus or couscous stay flagless forever. And on phones the section renders below the ingredients with a doubled rule above it, while on desktop its placement is right.

Finally, the recipe editor's most distinctive affordances — `#` section headings, `/`-linking another recipe — are documented nowhere. Users discover them by accident or not at all, and the docs site has no page on creating or editing recipes.

## Solution

A step gains **Step Ingredients**: references to the ingredient lines it uses, each carried as a fractional share of that line. Readers see the resolved names and amounts presented with the step — on the recipe page, in cooking mode, and on share pages — computed from the live ingredient line at the moment of display, so they are always correct for the active measurement system and follow every edit. The step's prose is never rewritten.

In the editor, typing `@` becomes a mention gesture: the autocomplete inserts the plain word into the sentence and attaches the ingredient to the step as a chip beneath it. Chips can also be added from a picker without mentioning anything in the text (that is how "add the spices" carries its three links), each chip can carry a fractional share, and chips are removed with a tap. The `@` never reaches the stored text, and the old inline linkification is retired outright — legacy tokens in existing recipes simply read as the literal text they always were on mobile.

**Ingredient Linking** joins the Recipe Enrichment family as its sixth kind, and it is a gap-filler in every case: automatic or manual, it only ever adds links to steps that have none, so it can never replace or remove what a person attached. There is deliberately no supplied-data suppression — the per-step check is the suppression, at the only granularity where it is true.

Recipe Provenance gets its three refinements. The stored country gains a written name produced by the same inference that writes the note, in the same language — a Dutch recipe about a Turkish dish is titled "Turkije"; a manual pick stores the name the editor chose in the picker, in their own words. Ambiguity stops being a reason for an empty country: the inference picks the single strongest claim and acknowledges rivals in the note, with null reserved for the genuinely unplaceable. And on phones the section moves to just after the cooking-mode control, before the ingredients, with the doubled rule fixed at its root: sections stop drawing their own leading separators, the page owns the rhythm between them.

The docs site gains two pages in the current Target Version, mirroring the shape the provenance page established: one on creating and editing recipes (basics, `#` sections, `/` recipe links, attaching ingredients while you type), and one on Step Ingredients end-to-end (what readers see, the admin switch, manual runs, what is never touched). Every screenshot is generated by the existing harness suite, not taken by hand.

## User Stories

1. As a cook, I want each step to show the ingredients it uses with their amounts, so that I never scroll back to the ingredient list mid-step.
2. As a cook, I want a step that aggregates ingredients — "add the spices" — to show every ingredient line it means, so that I know it is the salt, the pepper, and the paprika without guessing.
3. As a cook, I want a step that uses part of an ingredient — "add half the water" — to show the computed amount, so that 25 ml is written where I need it instead of arithmetic I do myself.
4. As a cook, I want step amounts to follow the active measurement system, so that the metric and US variants of a recipe are both right.
5. As a cook, I want cooking mode to show the current step's ingredients and amounts, so that the information is in front of me when my hands are full.
6. As a share-link recipient, I want steps on a shared recipe to show their ingredients and amounts, so that the recipe is as usable for me as for the household that owns it.
7. As a recipe editor, I want typing `@` to autocomplete an ingredient, insert the plain word into my sentence, and attach the ingredient to the step, so that linking costs nothing beyond typing.
8. As a recipe editor, I want to attach ingredients to a step from a picker without naming them in the text, so that "add the spices" can carry its three links.
9. As a recipe editor, I want to set a fractional share on an attached ingredient — half, a third, a quarter, or a custom value — so that partial use displays the right amount.
10. As a recipe editor, I want to remove or change a step's attached ingredients, so that a wrong link is mine to fix in place.
11. As a recipe editor, I want my step text stored exactly as I wrote it, so that linking never rewrites my recipe.
12. As a recipe editor, I want to ask AI to link the steps I have not linked, so that I can hand-link a few steps and have the rest filled in.
13. As a recipe editor, I want AI linking to leave every step that already has links untouched, so that my own work is never replaced by a rerun.
14. As a household administrator, I want an admin switch for automatic Ingredient Linking that ships off, so that upgrading never starts spending AI on its own.
15. As a recipe editor, I want a failed automatic linking run to stay quiet and a failed manual run to tell me, so that background AI is never my problem and requested AI never fails silently.
16. As a reader of a Dutch recipe for a Turkish dish, I want the provenance heading to say "Turkije", so that the heading speaks the language of the note beside it.
17. As a recipe editor picking a country by hand, I want the stored name to be the one I picked in the picker, so that supplied provenance stays in my own words.
18. As a cook, I want contested dishes to carry the single strongest origin country with rivals acknowledged in the note, so that a dish is never flagless just because history is argued.
19. As a cook, I want a genuinely unplaceable dish to keep an empty country rather than an invented one, so that provenance stays honest.
20. As a phone reader, I want provenance placed after the cooking-mode control and before the ingredients, so that where a dish comes from frames the recipe on mobile the way it does on desktop.
21. As a phone reader, I want exactly one rule between sections, so that the page never draws doubled lines.
22. As a new user, I want a docs page on creating and editing recipes — headings, linking other recipes, attaching ingredients to steps — so that the editor's affordances are discoverable.
23. As a new user, I want a docs page on Step Ingredients with real screenshots, so that I understand the amounts under steps and how AI fills them in.
24. As a self-hoster, I want each of these changes noted in the release notes for the Target Version, so that I know what an upgrade brings before I take it.

## Implementation Decisions

- A Step Ingredient is a reference from a step to one of the recipe's ingredient lines, carrying a fractional share (default one) and a display order. References live in measurement-system space like every other piece of recipe data: metric steps reference metric lines. Dual-system imports get the inference once, at the semantic level, fanned out to each system's rows by ingredient line order.
- Displayed amounts are always derived — share × the line's current amount — never stored. A line with no amount displays name only.
- References travel with the step payload on save, the same mechanism step images already use, so the fact that steps are recreated on save costs nothing. Deleting a step or an ingredient line deletes its references.
- The editor's `@` is a mention gesture, not syntax: it inserts the plain word and attaches a chip. Chips row per step with an add picker (the aggregate path) and per-chip share editing. The `@` is never persisted.
- Inline `@`-token linkification is deleted, with **no data migration**: legacy tokens in stored text render as the literal text they are. Decided explicitly; an ADR was offered and declined.
- Ingredient Linking is the sixth Recipe Enrichment kind under the standard contract — own queue, worker, admin-overridable prompt template, structured output schema, per-kind switch under the automatic-enrichment settings, entry in the recipe's actions menu, quiet automatic failure, visible manual failure. Automatic default: off.
- Its one deviation from the replacement-style kinds is deliberate: gap-filling for both origins, no supplied-data suppression at recipe level. Heading rows are never linked. No ingredients or no steps skips as insufficient input. A step that genuinely uses nothing stays bare and may be examined again by later runs.
- Recipe Provenance gains a stored written country name beside the ISO code. Inference writes it in the recipe's language (the skill it already demonstrates writing the note); a manual pick stores the picker label as shown to the picker. The code remains authoritative for flags. Rows with a code and no name fall back to the current endonym rendering, converging as runs happen. Flag tooltips, the dashboard, and the country picker remain reader-language chrome.
- The shipped default provenance prompt and schema change to: pick the single country with the strongest claim, acknowledge rivals in the note; null only when the dish belongs to no tradition at all. Admin prompt overrides are untouched.
- On the mobile recipe layout, provenance moves to after the cooking-mode control, before ingredients. Sections stop rendering their own leading separators; the page owns inter-section rules. The nutrition section gets the same ownership fix.
- Docs: two new pages in the Target Version — creating-and-editing, and step-ingredients — plus corrections to the three provenance-page sentences this work invalidates, and a release-notes section per feature. Five new screenshots, all generated by the harness screenshot suite.
- Delivery is one PR on the existing branch, commits ordered: test-mock cleanup in the provenance card tests, then the mobile layout fix, then the provenance refinements as one commit, then Step Ingredients in schema/backend → editor → reading-surfaces → e2e commits, then docs and screenshots.
- New user-facing strings land in every shipped locale.

## Testing Decisions

- A good test here asserts external behavior at the highest seam the repo has: the production-like AI harness that boots the real server and database and fakes only the AI provider's HTTP boundary. Assertions are on what a reader sees and what the database holds, never on internals. No new seams are introduced anywhere in this work.
- Ingredient Linking gets its own harness suite: an import followed by a manual run from the actions menu, covering the aggregate case (one step, several lines), the fractional case (half the water renders as the computed amount), gap-filling (a step with a hand-attached chip is untouched while bare steps are filled), and the reading surfaces (recipe page, cooking mode, share page).
- The provenance harness suite is extended with the recipe-language country name titling the card, and with the null path — an unplaceable dish leaving the section titled "Provenance" with nothing wiped — which today has no coverage at any level.
- Dense logic is tested one level down, each in the layer's established style: the worker's gap-filter and heading exclusion beside the existing enrichment worker tests; share-to-amount derivation and per-system resolution beside the existing shared-library tests; references riding the save payload and cascading deletes in the real-database repository tests; the mention gesture and chip editing beside the existing recipe-form component tests; prompt-content assertions beside the existing inferrer tests.
- The mobile page's component test asserts section order and exactly one separator between sections. The docs screenshot suite regenerates every embedded image, and the docs build validates the new pages.

## Out of Scope

- The mobile app rendering Step Ingredients. The data is server-side and ready; the native screens follow in a later effort, and until then mobile simply does not show links.
- Any migration of legacy `@` tokens. The retirement is renderer-only by explicit decision.
- Servings scaling. Fractional shares are scaling-ready by construction, but no scaling feature is built here.
- Recording the recipe's language. It stays deliberately unrecorded; the written country name is content, not a locale.
- Multiple origin countries, interactive cross-highlighting between steps and the ingredient list, and check-off synchronization in cooking mode.

## Further Notes

- This spec deliberately overturns one decision the original Recipe Provenance spec made on purpose: its user story 5 chose the endonym ("named as it names itself — 'Nederland', 'Italia'"). Cross-language cases — a Dutch recipe about a Turkish dish titled "Türkiye" — showed the heading belongs to the recipe's language, not the country's. The glossary entry for Recipe Provenance now records the content/chrome split.
- CONTEXT.md gained Step Ingredient and Ingredient Linking this session, and the Recipe Enrichment and Recipe Provenance entries were updated to match.
- Two provenance-card tests currently mock modules the component no longer uses and pass for the wrong reason; the first commit fixes them since this work touches that file anyway.
- The enrichment framework has two hand-maintained client-side state maps that do not fail compilation when a kind is missing; adding the sixth kind must include them, and they are the first place to look if lifecycle state looks stuck.
